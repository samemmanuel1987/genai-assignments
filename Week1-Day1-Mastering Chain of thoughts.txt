## Step 1: Clarify the exact failure signature
"Transaction could not be completed" is a generic, catch-all message — it doesn't point to a layer yet. Before anything else, gather:
- Exact error code (if any) shown alongside the message
- Channel used: mobile app, net banking, UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS
- Timestamp of the attempt
- Screenshot or exact wording, since banks often reuse this string for very different underlying faults

**Reasoning:** Different rails (UPI/IMPS/NEFT/RTGS) have different failure points, so identifying the rail narrows the search space immediately.

## Step 2: Check account-side status
- Was the amount **debited** from the sender's account or not? (Check statement/passbook, not just the app message)
- Is there a **hold** or **lien** on the account?
- Sufficient balance considering ₹50,000 + any transfer charges?
- Daily/per-transaction limit — many accounts cap UPI at ₹1 lakh/day but some apps cap single transactions lower (₹25k–₹50k for certain UPI apps)

**Reasoning:** This is the most common "discovery" — a debit with no credit (stuck in transit) is a completely different problem than a transaction that never left the sender's account.

## Step 3: Check beneficiary-side validity
- Is the beneficiary account active, correctly added, and IFSC/UPI ID valid?
- Recently added payee — some banks enforce a cooling period (24 hrs) with a lower transfer limit during that window
- Beneficiary bank undergoing maintenance/downtime at that time

## Step 4: Check network/gateway/timing factors
- Was this during a bank maintenance window (many Indian banks do nightly batch NEFT/RTGS windows)?
- Server timeout on the payment gateway (common cause of generic failure messages)
- Third-party UPI app issue vs. actual bank server issue (NPCI-side vs. bank-side)

## Step 5: Reconcile amount debited vs credited
- If debited but not credited: this typically **auto-reverses within T+1 working day** — check if that reversal happened
- If reversal hasn't happened past SLA: this becomes a formal complaint case

## Step 6: Cross-check with bank statement + reference/UTR number
- Every attempted transaction (success or fail) generates a UTR/RRN — pulling this lets the bank's backend team trace exactly where in the pipeline it stalled
